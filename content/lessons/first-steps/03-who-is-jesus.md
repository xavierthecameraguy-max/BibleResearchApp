Christianity is not first a set of rules or a philosophy. It is centred on a
person. So it is worth asking the question Jesus once put to his own friends: who
do you say I am? (Mark 8:29).

## A real person in history

Jesus was a real man who lived in a real place. He was born to a Jewish family,
probably a few years before the date we now call AD 1, and grew up in Nazareth, a
small village in Galilee. Most historians, whether Christian or not, agree that he
was a Jewish teacher, known as a healer, who gathered followers, and that he was
crucified under
the Roman governor Pontius Pilate around AD 30 to 33.

His name means "the Lord saves". "Christ" is not his surname. It is a title, from
the Greek word for [[messiah]], meaning "anointed one": the king God had promised
to send.

## Fully human

Jesus was not God pretending to be a man. He was truly human. He was born as a
baby and grew up (Luke 2:52). He got tired (John 4:6), hungry and thirsty. He wept
at a friend's grave (John 11:35). He was tempted in every way we are, yet he did
not sin (Hebrews 4:15).

This matters. It means Jesus understands your life from the inside. Your
tiredness, sadness and temptation are not foreign to him.

## Fully God

At the same time, Jesus did and said things that only God can do. He forgave sins,
which led religious leaders to ask who but God could do that (Mark 2:5-7). He
calmed a storm with a word (Mark 4:39-41). After his resurrection, his friend
Thomas fell before him and called him his Lord and his God (John 20:28).

John's Gospel opens by calling Jesus "the Word", who was with God and was God, and
who became flesh and lived among us (John 1:1, 14). Christians call this the
[[incarnation]]: God the Son taking on a full human life.

Our key passage, Philippians 2:5-11, may be one of the earliest Christian hymns. It
says that Jesus shared God's own nature, yet did not cling to his status. He
emptied himself, took the form of a servant, and humbled himself even to death on
a cross.

In AD 451 the Council of Chalcedon put this into careful words: Jesus is one
person who is both fully God and fully human. Catholic, Orthodox and most
Protestant churches accept this. The [[oriental-orthodox|Oriental Orthodox]]
churches, such as the Coptic and Armenian churches, word it differently, but they
too confess Jesus as truly God and truly human.

## His life

Jesus announced that the [[kingdom-of-god|kingdom of God]] had come near (Mark
1:15). He taught in memorable stories called [[parable|parables]]. He healed the
sick, welcomed people others pushed away, ate with outsiders, and called ordinary
people to be his [[disciple|disciples]]. His life showed what God's rule looks
like: love for God and love for neighbour, lived out completely.

## His death

Jesus was crucified, one of the cruellest punishments Rome used. But the New
Testament insists his death was not a tragic accident. He gave his life willingly,
as a ransom for many (Mark 10:45). Paul says simply that Christ died for our sins
(1 Corinthians 15:3).

The Bible uses several pictures to explain this, which Christians call the
[[atonement]]. Jesus is the [[sacrifice]] that deals with sin. He pays the ransom
that sets captives free. He is the victor who defeats evil and death. He takes on
himself what we deserved. Different traditions stress different pictures, but
most Christians hold several together, like the facets of one jewel.

## His resurrection

On the third day his tomb was found empty, and Jesus appeared alive to his
followers: to Peter, to the Twelve, and to more than five hundred people at once
(1 Corinthians 15:3-8). This was not a ghost or a dream. The Gospels show him
eating, talking and being touched.

The [[resurrection]] changes everything. It shows that Jesus' death really did
defeat sin and death, and it is a preview of what God will one day do for all his
people. After forty days Jesus ascended to the Father (Acts 1:9-11), and Christians
look forward to his return, the [[second-coming|second coming]].

## Lord and Saviour

Christians often call Jesus "Lord and Saviour". The two words belong together.

**Saviour** means he rescues us. We cannot fix the problem of sin by ourselves,
but he has done what we could not do.

**Lord** means he is the rightful king of our lives. One of the earliest Christian
statements of faith was simply "Jesus is Lord" (Romans 10:9). Our key passage ends
with every knee bowing and every tongue confessing that Jesus Christ is Lord
(Philippians 2:10-11).

Following Jesus as Lord is not grim duty. It is trusting that the one who gave his
life for you knows what is best for you.

## Getting to know him

The best way to know Jesus is to meet him in the Gospels. If you have not started
yet, try reading the Gospel of Mark, a chapter at a time. Watch what Jesus does,
listen to what he says, and ask: what does this show me about him? Bring what you
find to a pastor or mentor. There is always more to discover.

When you become a Christian, you are not just signing up to a set of ideas. You
are getting to know someone. So it makes sense to ask: who is this God I am now
following?

The Bible does not hand us a neat definition. Instead it tells a story in which
God shows us who he is by what he says and does. Still, a few big truths stand
out.

## One God

The Bible is clear that there is only one God. For thousands of years, faithful
Jews have prayed words from Deuteronomy 6:4, declaring that the Lord is one. Jesus
himself called this the most important commandment (Mark 12:29-30). Christians
did not abandon this belief. They hold it firmly.

## Father, Son and Holy Spirit

Yet as the story unfolds, something surprising appears. Look at our key passage,
Matthew 3:13-17. Jesus, the Son, is baptised in the river Jordan. As he comes up
out of the water, the Spirit of God comes down on him like a dove. And a voice
from heaven, the Father, says that this is his beloved Son, with whom he is well
pleased.

In one scene we meet the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit: each distinct, all
working together.

Later Jesus sends his followers to baptise people in the name of the Father and of
the Son and of the Holy Spirit (Matthew 28:19). Notice that it says "name", not
"names". Paul ends one of his letters with a blessing from all three (2
Corinthians 13:14).

Christians use the word [[trinity|Trinity]] to sum this up. The word itself is not
in the Bible, but it gathers up what the Bible shows. There is one God, who
eternally exists as three persons: the Father, the Son and the
[[holy-spirit|Holy Spirit]]. They are not three gods, and they are not one person
wearing three masks. Each is fully God, and together they are the one God.

In the fourth century, Christians put this into the Nicene [[creed]], which is
still said today in Catholic, Orthodox and many Protestant churches. It is one of
the beliefs that almost all Christians share.

## Is it all right if this is hard to understand?

Yes. People often try to explain the Trinity with pictures, such as water being
ice, liquid and steam. Every picture breaks down somewhere, and some accidentally
describe something different from what Christians believe. That should not
surprise us. If we could fully understand God, he would be no bigger than our
minds.

Here is the beautiful part. Because God is Father, Son and Spirit, love did not
begin when God made the world. Love has always existed within God, shared between
the Father, the Son and the Spirit. When the Bible says that God is love (1 John
4:8), it is describing his very nature. And the wonderful invitation of the
Christian faith is that we are drawn into that love.

## God is holy

The prophet Isaiah once saw a vision of God's throne, with heavenly beings
calling to each other, "Holy, holy, holy" (Isaiah 6:3). To be holy means to be set
apart: utterly pure and good, without any trace of evil. Isaiah's first reaction
was to realise how far short he fell. But God did not send him away. He cleansed
him and gave him a purpose (Isaiah 6:5-8).

God's holiness means he takes evil seriously. That is actually good news. A God
who shrugged at injustice and cruelty would not be good. And because of Jesus,
God's holiness is not something we need to run from. It is something he invites
us to share.

## God is loving

The best-known verse in the Bible says that God loved the world so much that he
gave his only Son (John 3:16). God's love is not a passing mood. It acts. John
explains that real love is not that we loved God, but that he loved us and sent
his Son to deal with our sins (1 John 4:9-10). You do not have to make God love
you. He already does.

## God is faithful

When Moses asked to see God's glory, God described himself as compassionate and
gracious, slow to anger, overflowing with steadfast love and faithfulness (Exodus
34:6). That description echoes through the rest of the Old Testament. Even in a
time of terrible grief, the writer of Lamentations could say that God's mercies
are new every morning (Lamentations 3:22-23).

Faithful means God keeps his promises. He does not change his mind about you when
you have a bad day.

## A note on calling God "Father"

For some people, the word "father" brings warm memories. For others it brings
pain. If your own father was absent, harsh or hurtful, it may be hard to think of
God this way. The Bible does not ask you to picture God as a copy of a human
father. Rather, God is the perfect Father that every human father falls short of.
If this is a struggle for you, it is worth talking about gently with a pastor,
mentor or counsellor.

## Getting to know him

You will spend the rest of your life getting to know God, and you will never run
out of things to discover. The best place to begin is with Jesus, because he shows
us what God is like (John 14:9). That is where we go in the next lesson.

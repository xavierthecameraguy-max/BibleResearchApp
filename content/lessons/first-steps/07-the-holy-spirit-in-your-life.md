Of the three persons of the Trinity, the Holy Spirit is often the one people find
hardest to picture. We can imagine a father. We can picture Jesus, who walked on
earth. But the Spirit can seem vague, like a force or a feeling.

The Bible paints a much richer picture.

## Who is the Holy Spirit?

The [[holy-spirit|Holy Spirit]] is not an "it". He is God, the third person of the
[[trinity|Trinity]], fully equal with the Father and the Son. The Bible describes
him doing personal things: he teaches, reminds, guides and speaks, and he can even
be grieved (John 14:26; Ephesians 4:30).

The Spirit was there at creation, hovering over the waters (Genesis 1:2). He
inspired the prophets. On the night before he died, Jesus promised to send the
Spirit as a helper and advocate who would be with his followers for ever (John
14:16-17).

Fifty days after Easter, at the festival of [[pentecost|Pentecost]], that promise
came true. The Spirit came on the first believers in a dramatic way, and the
church was born (Acts 2:1-4).

## What does the Spirit do?

The Spirit is active in every part of the Christian life. Here are some of the
things the New Testament says he does:

- **Gives new life.** Jesus said we must be born of the Spirit (John 3:5-8). The
  faith you now have is his work in you.
- **Assures you that you belong.** The Spirit helps us call God "Father" and
  confirms that we are his children (Romans 8:15-16).
- **Helps you pray.** When you do not know what to say, the Spirit helps (Romans
  8:26).
- **Guides you into truth.** Jesus said the Spirit would lead his followers into
  the truth (John 16:13).
- **Makes you more like Jesus.** Little by little, the Spirit changes us from the
  inside (2 Corinthians 3:18).
- **Joins believers together.** The Spirit makes Christians one body (1
  Corinthians 12:13).

## The fruit of the Spirit

Our key passage, Galatians 5:16-26, describes a struggle every Christian knows.
There is a pull towards selfishness, which Paul calls "the flesh", and the
Spirit's pull towards a new way of life. Paul's encouragement is simple: walk by
the Spirit and keep in step with him (Galatians 5:16, 25).

Then he describes what grows when the Spirit is at work. He calls it the fruit of
the Spirit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness,
gentleness and self-control (Galatians 5:22-23).

Notice the word "fruit". Fruit is not manufactured; it grows. An apple tree does
not strain to make apples. It stays healthy and connected to its roots, and fruit
appears in season. In the same way, these qualities grow as we stay close to God
through prayer, Scripture, worship and the company of other believers.

Notice too that Paul says "fruit", not "fruits". The Spirit does not give some
people love and others patience. He grows the whole character of Jesus in all his
people, slowly and patiently.

## The gifts of the Spirit

Alongside the fruit, the New Testament speaks of spiritual gifts. These are
abilities the Spirit gives to each believer to serve others and build up the
church (1 Corinthians 12:4-7; 1 Peter 4:10). The lists in Romans 12:6-8 and 1
Corinthians 12:8-10 include teaching, encouraging, serving, giving, leading,
showing mercy, wisdom, faith, healing, prophecy and speaking in tongues.

Two things are worth remembering. First, every Christian has gifts. You have
something to offer. Second, Paul says that without love even the most impressive
gifts are worth nothing (1 Corinthians 13:1-3). The gifts are tools; love is the
goal.

## Where Christians differ

Christians agree that the Spirit gives gifts. They differ on some of the more
unusual ones, such as speaking in tongues, prophecy and healing.

- **[[pentecostal|Pentecostal]] and charismatic** Christians believe all the gifts
  are for today and should be eagerly sought. Many speak of a "baptism in the
  Spirit", an experience of being filled with the Spirit's power, often with
  speaking in tongues. The charismatic movement is found in many denominations,
  including the Catholic Church.
- **Some Protestants**, often called cessationists, believe the more miraculous
  gifts had a special role in the time of the apostles and are not given in the
  same way today, though God still answers prayer for healing.
- **Catholic and [[orthodox|Orthodox]]** Christians teach that the Spirit is given
  in [[baptism]] and in confirmation, which the Orthodox call chrismation.
  Catholics also speak of seven gifts of the Spirit, drawn from Isaiah 11:2, such
  as wisdom, understanding and courage. Both traditions honour miracles in the
  lives of the saints and treasure the Spirit's work in prayer, worship and the
  [[sacrament|sacraments]]. They accept that God still gives unusual gifts, but
  generally urge humility, testing and caution about seeking such experiences.

Many Christians sit somewhere in between: open to whatever God may do, but careful
to test it (1 Thessalonians 5:19-21). If you are unsure, ask your pastor what your
church believes and why.

## Keep in step

You do not need to feel something dramatic to know the Spirit is with you. If you
trust Jesus, the Spirit is already at work in you: that trust is itself his gift.
Keep in step with him by praying,
reading Scripture, being honest about your struggles and spending time with other
believers. Over time you will see the fruit grow.

You have come a long way in these lessons, thinking about God, Jesus, grace,
prayer, the Bible, the Spirit, the church, baptism and communion. Now comes the
question every Christian lives with: how do I actually live this out, day by day?

## Your new life

Our key passage, Colossians 3:1-17, begins by reminding you who you are. You have
been raised with Christ, so set your heart on things above, where he is
(Colossians 3:1-2). Your life is now hidden with Christ in God (Colossians 3:3).

Then Paul uses a picture of changing clothes. Take off the old ways, like anger,
lying and selfishness (Colossians 3:8-9). Put on the new: compassion, kindness,
humility, gentleness and patience (Colossians 3:12). And over everything, like a
coat, put on love, which holds all the rest together (Colossians 3:14).

Notice the order. First Paul tells us who we are, then how to live. We do not
change in order to become God's children. We change because we already are.

## When temptation comes

Becoming a Christian does not make temptation disappear. Sometimes it can even feel
stronger, because you now care about things you did not care about before. That is
not a sign that something is wrong with your faith.

Jesus himself was tempted (Matthew 4:1-11), so he understands. Paul promises that
God will not let us be tempted beyond what we can bear, and will provide a way out
(1 Corinthians 10:13).

Some practical help:

- **Notice patterns.** When are you most tempted? When you are tired, lonely or
  stressed?
- **Plan a way out.** Decide in advance what you will do instead.
- **Pray in the moment.** A short prayer like "Jesus, help me" is enough.
- **Tell someone.** Struggles lose much of their power when brought into the
  light. A trusted Christian friend or mentor can pray with you (James 5:16).

## When you fall

You will fail sometimes. Every Christian does. The question is what you do next.

Do not hide from God, as Adam and Eve did in the garden. Run to him. If we confess
our sins, God is faithful and just to forgive (1 John 1:9). Feeling sorry for what
you have done is healthy when it leads you back to God. But a constant shame that
tells you God is tired of you does not come from him. [[repentance|Repentance]]
simply means turning around and coming home.

Many Christians, especially Catholic and Orthodox believers, confess to a priest in
the [[sacrament]] of confession, or reconciliation, and hear words of forgiveness
spoken aloud. Others confess privately to God, or with a trusted friend.
Traditions understand these differently, but all rest on God's promise to forgive.

Just as you have been forgiven, Paul asks you to forgive others (Colossians 3:13).
That can take time, especially if you have been deeply hurt. Forgiveness does not
mean pretending it did not matter, or that you must trust someone who is unsafe. If
you are struggling with this, talk to a pastor or counsellor.

## Loving your neighbour

When someone asked Jesus for the greatest commandment, he named two: love God with
all you are, and love your neighbour as yourself (Mark 12:29-31). When another man
asked who his neighbour was, Jesus told the story of the Good Samaritan, who cared
for a wounded stranger from a people his own people distrusted (Luke 10:25-37).

Loving your neighbour is usually not dramatic. It looks like listening well,
keeping your promises, being honest at work, helping with someone's shopping,
giving generously, standing up for someone treated unfairly, and being patient with
your family. These ordinary acts are signs of the [[kingdom-of-god|kingdom of God]].

## Sharing your faith gently

You may want to tell others about Jesus. That is a good desire. Peter encourages
believers to be ready to explain the hope they have, with gentleness and respect (1
Peter 3:15-16).

You do not need to win arguments or have every answer. Start with your own story:
what life was like, how you came to trust Jesus, and what difference he is making.
Listen more than you speak. Pray for friends by name. Invite them to church, or to
read a Gospel with you. And remember that changing hearts is God's work, not yours.
Your part is simply to be honest and kind.

## Growing takes time

Spiritual growth is more like a tree than a light switch. There will be seasons
when God feels close and seasons when he feels far away. There may be days of
doubt. That is normal. One father in the Gospels cried out, "I believe; help my
unbelief!" (Mark 9:24), and Jesus helped him.

Paul was confident that the God who began a good work in you will carry it on to
completion (Philippians 1:6). You are not finished, and that is fine. God is
patient with you. Be patient with yourself.

Prayer can feel intimidating at first. What words should I use? Am I doing it
right? Will God even listen?

Here is the good news: prayer is simply talking with God. You do not need special
words, a special place or a special voice. You can pray out loud or silently,
kneeling, sitting, walking or lying in bed. God already knows your heart. He just
wants you to come.

## Jesus' advice on prayer

In our key passage, Matthew 6:5-15, Jesus gives some simple guidance. Do not pray
to impress other people (Matthew 6:5). Do not think you need to pile up lots of
words (Matthew 6:7). Your Father knows what you need before you ask (Matthew 6:8).

Then Jesus gives his followers a model prayer. We call it the Lord's Prayer, and
Christians all over the world have prayed it for two thousand years.

## The Lord's Prayer as a pattern

You can pray the Lord's Prayer word for word, and many Christians do every day. You
can also use it as a pattern, praying through each line in your own words.

- **"Our Father in heaven."** Begin by remembering who you are talking to: a loving
  Father who is also the great God of heaven. Notice the word "our". Even when you
  pray alone, you pray as part of a family.
- **"Hallowed be your name."** "Hallowed" means "honoured as holy". Praise God for
  who he is.
- **"Your kingdom come, your will be done."** Ask for God's good rule to grow in
  your life, your family, your town and the world.
- **"Give us today our daily bread."** Bring your everyday needs to him, big and
  small.
- **"Forgive us, as we forgive others."** Be honest about where you have gone
  wrong, and ask for help to forgive those who have hurt you.
- **"Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil."** Ask for strength
  and protection.

## A simple pattern: ACTS

Some people find it helpful to remember four letters, ACTS:

- **Adoration:** praising God for who he is.
- **Confession:** telling God honestly where you have fallen short (1 John 1:9).
- **Thanksgiving:** thanking him for what he has done, including small, everyday
  gifts.
- **Supplication:** an old word for asking, for yourself and for others.

This is not a rule. It is just scaffolding for the days when you are not sure
where to start.

## Be honest

You do not have to tidy up your feelings before you pray. Read the Psalms and you
will find people shouting for joy, crying in grief, asking God "How long?" (Psalm
13:1) and even complaining. Prayers like these are called [[lament|laments]], and
God welcomes them. He would rather hear your real questions than your polite
pretending.

And when you have no words at all, Paul says the Holy Spirit helps us in our
weakness and prays for us when we do not know what to say (Romans 8:26).

## Praying with others

Prayer is personal, but it is not only private. The first Christians prayed
together constantly (Acts 1:14; Acts 2:42). Jesus promised to be present where two
or three gather in his name (Matthew 18:20).

Praying aloud in a group can feel nerve-racking at first. It is fine to start by
listening and saying "Amen", which means "yes, may it be so". Short, simple
prayers are welcome. No one is grading you.

## Different ways Christians pray

Christians in different traditions have developed rich ways of praying, and you
may find help in more than one.

- **Set prayers.** Many Christians use written prayers that others have prayed for
  centuries. Catholics often pray the Rosary, which reflects on events in the life
  of Jesus. Orthodox Christians use prayer books with morning and evening prayers,
  and many repeat the Jesus Prayer: "Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on
  me, a sinner." Anglicans treasure the Book of Common Prayer. Written prayers can give you words when your own run dry.
- **[[liturgy|Liturgy]].** In Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, Lutheran and some
  other churches, public worship follows a set pattern of prayers, readings and
  responses. Praying the same words together week after week can shape your heart
  over time.
- **Spontaneous prayer.** In many evangelical, Pentecostal and other churches,
  people mostly pray in their own words, as they feel led. This can feel very
  personal and immediate.

None of these is the only right way. Set prayers can be deeply heartfelt, and
spontaneous prayers can be deeply thoughtful. Ask a pastor or mentor how they
pray, and try a few approaches.

## Start small

You do not need to pray for an hour. Try a few minutes each morning or evening.
You might use the prayer journal in this app to note what you are praying for, and
come back later to record how God answered. Over time you may be surprised by how
much you have to thank him for.

Welcome. If you have recently said yes to Jesus, whether in a big moment at a
church service, quietly on your own, or after a long season of questions, this
lesson is for you.

You may be feeling joy, relief or simply curiosity. You may also feel a little
lost. That is completely normal. Becoming a Christian is a beginning, not a
finish line, and nobody expects you to understand everything yet. These ten short
lessons are here to help you find your feet.

## The good news in a few sentences

The word [[gospel]] means "good news". Here it is in simple terms.

- **God made us for himself.** He made the world good, and he made people to
  know him, love him and reflect his goodness.
- **We turned away.** All of us have gone our own way, in big and small ways.
  The Bible calls this [[sin]]. It damages our relationship with God, with each
  other and with ourselves, and we cannot fix it on our own (Romans 3:23).
- **God came to rescue us.** God did not leave us there. He came to us in
  Jesus, who lived the life we could not live, died on the cross for our sins,
  and rose from the dead on the third day (1 Corinthians 15:3-4).
- **We receive it by trusting him.** Forgiveness and new life are a gift. We
  receive that gift by turning to God, which the Bible calls [[repentance]], and
  by putting our trust, or [[faith]], in Jesus. Many churches also teach that
  God gives this new life in [[baptism]], which Lesson 9 explores.

That is the heart of it. Everything else in the Christian life grows from these
roots.

## What Paul says has happened

Our key passage, 2 Corinthians 5:17-21, is one of the clearest pictures in the
Bible of what happens when someone comes to Christ. Paul packs three big ideas
into five verses.

**A new start.** Anyone who is in Christ, Paul writes, is a
[[new-creation|new creation]]. The old has gone and the new has come (2
Corinthians 5:17). This does not mean your personality vanishes, or that every
problem disappears overnight. It means God has begun something new in you, and
he promises to finish it (Philippians 1:6).

**A restored friendship.** Paul's key word here is "reconciled". It describes two
people who were estranged becoming friends again. Notice who moves first: all of
this, Paul says, comes from God (2 Corinthians 5:18). God was not waiting for us
to tidy ourselves up. He came looking for us.

**A great exchange.** Verse 21 is one of the most remarkable sentences in the
Bible. Jesus, who had no sin, took our sin on himself, so that we could share in
his goodness and be put right with God. Christians have sometimes called this
"the great exchange". You bring him your sin and brokenness; he gives you his
welcome and his life. That is [[grace]]: a gift we could never earn.

## You have been welcomed into a family

The New Testament often describes becoming a Christian as being adopted. Those
who receive Jesus are given the right to become children of God (John 1:12).
God's [[holy-spirit|Holy Spirit]] lives in believers and helps them call God
"Father" (Romans 8:15-16).

This means you now have brothers and sisters all over the world and across two
thousand years of history. Some of them worship in ancient cathedrals, some in
village chapels, some in school halls and living rooms. Together they are what
the Bible calls the [[church]]: not first a building, but a people.

## How did it happen for you?

People come to faith in many ways. Some can name the day and the hour. Others
came to trust Jesus slowly, over months or years. Some were baptised as babies
and grew up in church, and only now is the faith they were given becoming truly
their own.
Christian traditions describe these beginnings in different ways, and later
lessons will explain some of those differences fairly.

What matters most is not how dramatic your story feels, but who you are
trusting. Feelings come and go. God's promises stay the same.

## You are not alone

Becoming a Christian does not mean you suddenly have to be perfect, or that you
must work everything out by yourself. Jesus promised his followers that he would
be with them always (Matthew 28:20). You also have the help of other believers.

If you have not already, try to find someone further along the road: a pastor, a
priest, a mentor or a trusted Christian friend. Tell them you are new to this and
would love some help. Most will be delighted to walk alongside you. Questions are
welcome, and doubts are not a sign of failure. The first disciples had plenty of
both.

## What is coming in this track

Over the next nine lessons we will look at who God is, who Jesus is, what grace
means, how to pray, how to read the Bible, the work of the Holy Spirit, finding a
church family, baptism and communion, and how to live all of this out day by day.
Each lesson is short, ends with a few questions to talk over, and suggests one
small step to try.

Take your time. The quizzes are only there to help things stick, not to grade
you. You are already loved, and you are already home.

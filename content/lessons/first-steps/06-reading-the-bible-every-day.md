The Bible is one of the main ways God speaks to his people. Christians believe God
[[inspiration|inspired]] its writers, so that through their words we hear his voice (2 Timothy
3:16). Reading it regularly is one of the best habits you can build as a new
believer.

But the Bible is a big book, and it can be hard to know where to begin. This
lesson offers a simple way to start.

## Why read every day?

Our key passage, Psalm 1, describes a person who delights in God's teaching and
thinks about it day and night. That person is like a tree planted beside streams
of water. It bears fruit in its season, and its leaves do not wither (Psalm 1:3).

Trees do not grow by trying hard. They grow by staying near water. Reading
Scripture regularly keeps you near the source of life. You will not notice growth
every day, but over months and years, roots go deep.

The psalm talks about delighting in God's [[law]], which in Hebrew is
[[torah]]. It means much more than rules. It is God's instruction: the guidance of
a loving Father.

## Where to start

You do not have to start at Genesis and read straight through. The Bible is a
library of many books, written in different styles over many centuries. Some are
much easier for a beginner than others.

A great place to start is one of the Gospels, which tell the story of Jesus.

- **Mark** is the shortest Gospel, fast-moving and full of action. At a chapter a
  day you can read it in just over two weeks.
- **John** is more reflective. John says he wrote so that readers would believe
  Jesus is the Messiah, the Son of God, and find life in his name (John 20:31).

After that, you might read Acts, the story of the early church, a short letter
like Philippians, or some of the Psalms.

This app has reading plans that can help. "The Gospels in 30 days" walks you
through all four Gospels. "The Big Story in 40 days" takes you through key
chapters from Genesis to Revelation, following the lessons in The Big Story track.
Choose one that fits your life. If a plan feels like too much, simply read one
chapter of Mark a day.

## A simple way to read

You do not need to be a scholar. Try these four steps:

1. **Pray.** Ask God to help you understand and to speak to you.
2. **Read.** Read a short passage, perhaps a chapter or less. Read it slowly,
   maybe twice.
3. **Notice.** Ask a few questions. What does this show me about God? About Jesus?
   About people? Is there a promise to trust, a command to follow or an example to
   learn from?
4. **Respond.** Talk to God about what you read. Thank him, ask him for help, or
   tell him what puzzled you.

Some people like to write a sentence or two in a notebook or in the app's notes.
Others highlight a verse to remember. Find what works for you.

## When it is confusing

You will come across passages that are hard to understand, and some that are
troubling. That is normal. The Bible was written long ago, in other languages and
cultures. Do not be discouraged.

Make a note of your question and bring it to a pastor, mentor or small group. Look
up unfamiliar words in the glossary. The track How to Read the Bible explains
[[context]] and [[genre]], which clear up many puzzles. Wherever you can, read the
hard passages in the light of the clearer ones, and above all in the light of
Jesus.

## Read with others

Reading on your own is valuable, but reading with others is a gift too. Acts 17:11
describes believers in Berea who eagerly examined the Scriptures every day. Many
churches have small groups or Bible studies where people read a passage together
and share what they notice. Others will see things you miss, and you will see
things they miss.

Christians also hear the Bible together in worship. Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican,
Lutheran and many other churches follow a lectionary, a set pattern of readings
through the year. Hearing Scripture read aloud with your church family is one of
the oldest ways Christians have received God's word.

## Missed a day? That's fine

Habits take time. If you miss a day, or a week, you have not failed. Just pick up
again. This is not about ticking boxes. It is about getting to know God. A few
minutes on most days is far better than a long session once in a while.

## A promise to hold

Psalm 119:105 calls God's word a lamp for our feet and a light for our path. A
lamp does not show you the whole road at once. It shows you enough for the next
step. That is often how God's word works in our lives.

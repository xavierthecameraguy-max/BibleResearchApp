Last time we saw the world as God made it: good, ordered, full of life, with
humans made in God's image to care for it. The first two chapters of Genesis end
in a garden, with the man and the woman living openly with God and with each
other, "naked and not ashamed" (Genesis 2:25).

But you only have to watch the news, or look honestly at your own heart, to know
that the world is not like that now. Genesis 3 explains how things went wrong.
Christians often call this moment "the Fall".

## One tree, one boundary

In the garden God gives the humans enormous freedom. They can eat from any tree
except one: the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 2:16-17). God warns
that eating from it will lead to death.

Why one forbidden tree? It seems to stand for a simple truth: God is God, and we
are not. Only God has the right to decide what is good and what is evil. The
humans are invited to trust him on that, and to receive his wisdom as a gift.

## The question that started it all

Into the garden comes a crafty serpent. Later parts of the Bible connect this
figure with the devil, a spiritual enemy of God. The serpent does not start with
a lie. It starts with a question: did God really say you cannot eat from any
tree? (Genesis 3:1).

It is a clever twist. God had been generous, but the serpent makes him sound
stingy. Then it goes further, telling the woman she will not die, and that God is
holding back because he knows eating will make them like him, knowing good and
evil.

The woman looks at the fruit. It looks good, and it seems to offer wisdom. She
eats, and gives some to her husband, who was with her, and he eats too (Genesis
3:6). At its core, this is a choice about trust. Will they believe God is good and
wise, or will they take control and decide for themselves?

The Bible has a word for this: [[sin]]. Sin is more than breaking a rule. It is
missing the mark of what we were made for, turning from the God who loves us to
go our own way.

## Everything breaks

The results come quickly.

- **Shame.** Their eyes are opened and they feel exposed. They sew fig leaves
  to cover themselves (Genesis 3:7).
- **Hiding.** When God comes walking in the garden, they hide (Genesis 3:8).
  The friendship between God and people is damaged.
- **Blame.** When God asks what happened, the man blames the woman (and God, for
  giving her to him), and the woman blames the serpent (Genesis 3:12-13).
  Relationships between people are damaged.
- **A groaning world.** Childbirth will be painful, marriage will be marked by
  struggle, and work will be hard as the ground produces thorns (Genesis
  3:16-19). Even the relationship between humans and the earth is damaged.
- **Death.** Finally, they are sent out of the garden, away from the tree of
  life (Genesis 3:22-24). Death has entered the story.

Notice how wide the damage spreads. Sin is not just a private problem between me
and God. It affects families, communities and the natural world. That is why
the Bible's answer, when it comes, will be big enough to heal all of it.

## Hope in the middle of judgement

Here is the surprising part. Even in this sad chapter, God does not give up on
his people.

First, he comes looking for them. "Where are you?" he calls (Genesis 3:9). God
already knows where they are. The question is an invitation to come out and be
honest.

Second, he cares for them. Before they leave the garden, God makes them clothes
from animal skins to replace their flimsy fig leaves (Genesis 3:21). It is a
small, tender act of [[grace]], God's kindness that we do not deserve.

Third, and most importantly, he makes a promise. Speaking to the serpent, God
says there will be ongoing hostility between the serpent and the woman, and
between their offspring. One day the woman's offspring will crush the serpent's
head, even though the serpent will strike his heel (Genesis 3:15).

On its own the verse is short and mysterious. But Christians from very early on
have seen it as the first glimmer of the [[gospel]], the good news. A descendant
of the woman will one day defeat evil, though he will be wounded in the process.
The New Testament presents Jesus as that victor, who defeats evil through his
own suffering. Because of this, some Christians call Genesis 3:15 the "first
gospel".

## Different ways Christians explain the Fall

Christians agree that sin is real, that it affects everyone, and that we cannot
fix it on our own. They describe how it passes down in slightly different ways.
Catholic and many Protestant teaching speaks of "original sin", a fallen
condition we all inherit. Orthodox Christians often prefer to speak of "ancestral
sin". They stress that we inherit mortality and a damaged nature rather than
Adam's personal guilt, and that each person is responsible for their own sins. Christians also differ on how literally to read the details of this
chapter. But all agree it tells the truth about us: something is badly wrong,
and we need rescue.

## Where we go next

Genesis 3 is honest about why life hurts. But it ends with a door left open. God
is still involved, still caring, still promising. In the next lesson we will see
how sin spreads across the early world in the stories of Cain, Noah and the
tower of Babel, and how God keeps choosing to work with people rather than
abandon them.

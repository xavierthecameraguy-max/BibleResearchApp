Last time we reached the centre of the story: Jesus' death and resurrection, the
coming of the Spirit at Pentecost, and the good news spreading to Jews and
Gentiles across the world. But the world still has sickness, injustice and death.
So how does the story end?

## A surprising ending

Many people imagine that the Christian hope is to die and float away to heaven,
leaving this world behind. Christians do believe that those who die in Christ
are with him (Philippians 1:21-23). But the Bible's final hope is bigger and
more earthy than that.

The last book of the Bible is Revelation, written by a man named John while
exiled on the island of Patmos, probably near the end of the first century AD (a few scholars prefer the late
60s). It
is [[apocalyptic]] writing, a type of literature that uses vivid symbols,
visions and numbers to pull back the curtain on God's view of history. Christians
interpret many of its details in different ways, and some passages are puzzling
even for scholars. You do not need to decode every beast and number. Its central
message is clear: God and the Lamb (Jesus) defeat evil, and God makes all things
new.

## Heaven comes to earth

In Revelation 21, John sees a new heaven and a new earth. Then he sees the holy
city, a new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God (Revelation 21:1-2).

Notice the direction. People do not go up to God. God comes down to live with
his people. A loud voice announces that God's dwelling place is now with people,
and he will live with them (Revelation 21:3). He will wipe every tear from their
eyes, and there will be no more death, grief, crying or pain. The one on the
throne says that he is making everything new (Revelation 21:4-5).

This is the [[new-creation|new creation]]. It does not mean God throws away the
old world and starts again from nothing. It means he heals and renews it, the way
Jesus' resurrected body was still his body, but transformed. (Some Christians,
reading fiery passages such as 2 Peter 3:10-13, expect more of a fresh start,
but Catholic, Orthodox and many Protestant traditions speak of renewal.) The
[[resurrection]] of Jesus was the first piece of this new world, arriving
early.

## Back to the garden, and beyond

The final vision is full of echoes from the beginning of the story.

- **A river and a tree.** A river of the water of life flows from God's throne,
  and on either side stands the tree of life, last seen in Eden (Revelation
  22:1-2). Its leaves are for the healing of the nations.
- **No more curse.** In Genesis 3 the ground was cursed. Now there will no longer
  be any curse (Revelation 22:3).
- **Face to face.** The first humans hid from God. Now his people will see his face
  (Revelation 22:4).
- **Ruling again.** In Genesis 1 humans were made to care for creation. Now God's
  people reign with him forever (Revelation 22:5).

But it is not just a return to the garden. The garden has become a city, full of
people from every nation, tribe and language (Revelation 7:9). The scattered
nations of Babel are gathered into one family, just as God promised Abraham.

There is something else missing from the city: a temple. John says he saw no
temple, because God himself and the Lamb are its temple (Revelation 21:22). The
whole city is shaped like a perfect cube (Revelation 21:16), like the Most Holy
Place in Solomon's temple (1 Kings 6:20). The tabernacle and the temple were always small previews. Now the whole
creation is filled with God's presence.

## The whole story in one view

Now that we have reached the end, step back and look at the whole Bible:

1. **Creation.** God makes a good world and humans in his image to live with him.
2. **Fall.** Humans turn away, and sin and death break everything.
3. **Promise.** God chooses Abraham's family to bring blessing to all nations.
4. **Israel.** He rescues them in the exodus, gives them his Law, lives among them,
   and promises a King from David's line. Yet they keep failing, and go into exile.
5. **Jesus.** The promised King arrives, announces the kingdom, dies for sin and
   rises from the dead.
6. **The church.** The Spirit sends his people to carry the good news to every
   nation.
7. **New creation.** God renews all things and lives with his people forever.

The [[covenant|covenants]] with Noah, Abraham, Israel and David, and the new
covenant in Jesus, are like chapters that move the story along. The theme of God
dwelling with his people, from Eden to the tabernacle, the temple, Jesus and the
Spirit-filled church, finds its fulfilment here.

## Living in the story now

Where are we in this story? We live between Jesus' resurrection and his return
(the [[second-coming|second coming]]), in part six. The [[kingdom-of-god|kingdom of God]] has begun, but it is not
yet complete. Many Christians call this the "already and not yet".

That changes how we live. If God were simply going to throw the world away, it
would be easy to think nothing here matters much. But if God is renewing it, then what we do now matters a great deal.
Paul, after a long chapter on resurrection, tells believers that their work for
the Lord is never wasted (1 Corinthians 15:58). Every act of kindness, honesty,
forgiveness, justice and care is a small sign of the world to come.

It also gives hope in suffering. If you are grieving or struggling, the last page
of the Bible is a promise written for you: tears will be wiped away, and death
will be gone.

The Bible's final chapter ends with an invitation. The Spirit and the bride, the
church, say "Come", and anyone who is thirsty is welcome to drink freely from the
water of life (Revelation 22:17). And it ends with a prayer Christians have
prayed ever since: come, Lord Jesus (Revelation 22:20).

You probably know people who follow another religion, or none at all. They may be your neighbours, your colleagues, your closest friends or your own family. Perhaps you were one of them until recently. As a new Christian, you may be wondering: are all religions basically the same? What happens to people who never hear about Jesus? And how should I treat friends who believe differently?

These are big questions, and Christians have answered them in more than one way. This lesson is an introduction, not the last word.

## Our neighbours' faiths

A few lines cannot do justice to any religion, but here is a respectful starting point. The best way to learn more is to ask a friend what their faith means to them.

- **Judaism** is Christianity's closest relative. Jesus and his first followers were Jews, and Christians share the Hebrew Scriptures, which they call the Old Testament. Jewish people worship the God of Abraham and treasure the [[torah|Torah]], but do not accept Jesus as the Messiah.
- **Islam**, the world's second-largest religion, teaches that there is one God and that Muhammad is his final prophet. Muslims honour Jesus as a great prophet and the Messiah, born of the virgin Mary, but not as the Son of God, and most believe he was not crucified.
- **Hinduism** is a very old and varied family of traditions from India, with many ways of understanding the divine.
- **Buddhism** goes back to Siddhartha Gautama, known as the Buddha, who taught in northern India, probably in the fifth or fourth century BC (scholars debate the exact dates). It offers a path to freedom from suffering, and many Buddhists do not focus on a creator God.
- **Many other traditions**, such as Sikhism and the traditional religions of Africa, Asia and the Americas, are followed by millions of people.
- **People of no religion** make up a large and growing part of the world. Many are thoughtful, generous people with serious reasons for their views.

Religions share some things, such as prayer, compassion and concern for right living. They also disagree on important questions, such as who Jesus is and whether he rose from the dead. Taking those differences seriously is part of respecting one another. Saying that all religions teach the same thing can overlook what people actually believe.

## What do Christians believe about salvation?

The New Testament makes a bold claim. Jesus says that he is the way, the truth and the life, and that no one comes to the Father except through him (John 14:6). Peter tells the authorities in Jerusalem that [[salvation|salvation]] is found in no one else (Acts 4:12). At the same time, the Bible speaks of God's love for the whole world. God wants all people to be saved (1 Timothy 2:4). He has not left himself without witness among the nations (Acts 14:17), and Peter discovers that God accepts people from every nation who fear him and do what is right (Acts 10:34-35).

Christians hold these truths together in different ways:

- **[[exclusivism|Exclusivism]]** holds that salvation comes only through Jesus and only to those who consciously trust in him. Many evangelical and Pentecostal Protestants hold this view. It gives a strong sense of urgency to [[missions]]: how will people believe if no one tells them (Romans 10:14)? Many who hold it also trust God's mercy for those, such as young children, who die before they can respond.
- **[[inclusivism|Inclusivism]]** holds that salvation comes only through Christ, but that God may save some who never clearly heard of him, according to how they responded to the light they had (compare Romans 2:14-16). The Catholic Church, especially since the Second Vatican Council (1962-1965), teaches that people who, through no fault of their own, do not know the gospel, but who sincerely seek God and, moved by his grace, follow their conscience, may be saved. Catholics add that all salvation comes from Christ through the Church, which is his Body. Many Protestants, including C. S. Lewis, have held similar hopes.
- **Orthodox Christians** teach that salvation is found in Christ and his Church, and are often reluctant to say more. A well-known Orthodox saying, made popular by Bishop Kallistos Ware, runs: we know where the Church is, but we cannot say where it is not. They entrust others to the mercy of God.

Another view, pluralism, says that all religions are equally valid paths to God. Most Protestant, Catholic and Orthodox churches do not accept it, because it sets aside what the New Testament says about Jesus. Many followers of other religions do not accept it either, because it tends to blur what makes their own faith distinctive.

Whatever their view, Christians agree on two things. Salvation is God's gift through Jesus, and God, not us, is the judge. Abraham's question is a good one to rest on: will not the Judge of all the earth do what is right? (Genesis 18:25).

## Loving our neighbours

Jesus told a story about a man beaten and left for dead (Luke 10:25-37). The hero was a Samaritan, from a people Jesus' listeners looked down on and whose worship differed from theirs. Jesus' point was clear: love your neighbour, whoever they are.

Paul's visit to Athens offers a model too. Paul was troubled by the city's many idols, but he did not mock. He walked around and paid attention. He noticed an altar to an unknown god and used it as a starting point (Acts 17:23). He quoted the Athenians' own poets. Then he spoke clearly about Jesus and the resurrection. Some scoffed, some wanted to hear more, and a few believed (Acts 17:32-34).

Peter adds that we should always be ready to explain our hope, but with gentleness and respect (1 Peter 3:15-16). That is the heart of good [[apologetics]]. In practice, it might mean:

- Being a real friend, not treating people as projects.
- Asking questions and listening, so you learn what your friends actually believe.
- Sharing your own story honestly when there is an opening.
- Standing up for the freedom and safety of people of every faith.

If a friendship raises questions you are unsure how to handle, talk them over with a pastor or mentor. And keep praying for your friends. God loves them even more than you do.

At some point, almost every Christian wonders: what if none of this is real? You cannot see God or measure him in a laboratory. The excitement of your first weeks of faith may have faded, and friends may tell you that belief in God is just wishful thinking.

If that is where you are, you have not failed. As the first lesson in this track showed, honest questions have a place in faith. This lesson looks at how Christians answer this one, and why the answer involves more than a single argument.

## Different ways of knowing

We know things in different ways: maths by reasoning, science by testing, history through trustworthy witnesses, and a friend by spending time with them and seeing them keep their word.

Knowing God, Christians believe, involves all of these, but is most like knowing a person. Evidence matters, but God is someone to be met, not only a theory to be proved.

## Reasons people give

Christians have long offered thoughtful reasons for their faith, a practice called [[apologetics]]. Peter encouraged believers to be ready to explain their hope, with gentleness and respect (1 Peter 3:15). Here are some of those reasons:

- **Why is there anything at all?** Most scientists think the universe as we know it began roughly 13.8 billion years ago. Many people think that something which begins must have a cause beyond itself. The Bible says the heavens declare God's glory (Psalm 19:1), and that his power can be seen in what he has made (Romans 1:20).
- **A universe that fits.** The universe follows orderly laws that our minds can understand, and some of its basic settings seem finely balanced to allow life. Many people, scientists among them, see this as a sign of design. Others suggest explanations such as the possibility of many universes.
- **Right and wrong.** Nearly everyone senses that some things, like cruelty to a child, are truly wrong, not merely unpopular. C. S. Lewis argued that this moral sense points to a moral lawgiver beyond us.
- **Deep longing.** Humans long for meaning, beauty and a love that lasts. Augustine, writing around AD 400, told God that our hearts are restless until they rest in him. Ecclesiastes says that God has set eternity in the human heart (Ecclesiastes 3:11).
- **Jesus himself.** For most Christians the strongest reason is Jesus: his life, his teaching, and the evidence for his resurrection, which earlier lessons in this track explored.

It is only fair to say that many thoughtful people are not persuaded. Atheist and agnostic thinkers offer other explanations for the universe and for our moral sense, and many point to suffering as a reason to doubt. People of other faiths understand God differently. These arguments do not force anyone to believe, but they can show that faith in God is reasonable, not a leap in the dark. The 17th-century French scientist Blaise Pascal suggested that God gives enough light for those who seek him, but not so much that anyone is forced to believe.

## Experience and the Spirit

Many Christians describe experiences of God: a prayer answered unexpectedly, deep peace in a hard season, a sense of his presence in worship, or a habit that finally lost its grip. Many, especially Catholic and Orthodox believers, also speak of meeting Christ in the [[sacrament|sacraments]], such as Communion. Paul writes that the [[holy-spirit|Holy Spirit]] assures believers, deep within, that they are God's children (Romans 8:15-16).

Experience is not everything. Feelings come and go, and experiences can be misread. People of other religions report powerful experiences too. That is why Christians weigh experiences against Scripture and talk them over with others (1 John 4:1).

Some of the most faithful Christians in history went through long seasons when God felt absent. The 16th-century Spanish friar and poet John of the Cross wrote of a "dark night" of the soul. Letters published after Mother Teresa died revealed that for decades she felt God was distant, yet she kept praying and serving. Feeling far from God is not the same as God being far from you.

## You are not meant to do this alone

Faith grows best in company. When you see God at work in other people's lives and experience the love of a church family, your own trust grows stronger. Jesus said the world would recognise his followers by their love for one another (John 13:35).

When doubts come, do not hide them. Share them with a pastor, priest, mentor or small group. Someone else has almost certainly asked the same question.

## Faith as trust

In the Bible, [[faith]] is not believing something without any reasons, and it is not forcing yourself to feel certain. It is trust. Think of crossing a bridge. You look at it carefully, you see that others have crossed it safely, and then you step out. Faith is the walking.

You do not need perfect certainty to follow Jesus. Remember the father who cried out, "I believe; help my unbelief!" (Mark 9:24). Jesus did not turn him away.

## Keep seeking

Speaking in Athens, Paul told his listeners that God made people so that they would seek him and reach out for him, though he is not far from any of us (Acts 17:27). Through Jeremiah, God told his people in exile that they would find him when they sought him with all their heart (Jeremiah 29:13). Jesus promised that those who seek will find (Matthew 7:7).

Here are some ways to keep seeking:

- Keep praying, even when it feels like talking to the ceiling.
- Read the Gospels slowly, and get to know Jesus.
- Stay connected to a church, and serve others alongside its people.
- Explore the other tracks in this app, from First Steps and The Big Story to How We Got the Bible, How We Got Here and How to Read the Bible.

Above all, remember that the search is not only yours. In Luke 15, Jesus tells of a shepherd who searches for one lost sheep, and a father who runs to welcome home his lost son. Christians believe that long before you started looking for God, he was already looking for you.

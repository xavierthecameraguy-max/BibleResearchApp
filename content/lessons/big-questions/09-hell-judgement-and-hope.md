Few topics raise as many questions, or as much fear, as hell. Perhaps someone once used it to frighten you. Perhaps you are worried about a friend or family member who died without sharing your faith.

If this subject touches a fresh grief or a deep fear, go gently, and please talk it over with a pastor, priest or trusted mentor.

## Judgement can be good news

We often think of judgement only as something to dread, but in the Bible it is often a reason for joy. Psalm 96 pictures the fields and trees singing because God is coming to set things right (Psalm 96:11-13). For people who have suffered injustice, the promise that evil will not have the last word is a deep comfort. A world where cruelty was never answered would not be a good world. Trusting God to judge also frees us to leave revenge to him (Romans 12:17-19).

In the ancient [[creed|creeds]], Christians confess that Jesus will [[second-coming|come again]] to judge the living and the dead. And notice who the Judge is: Jesus, the one who loved sinners enough to die for them (John 5:22; Acts 17:31).

## What the Bible says about hell

In English Bibles, "hell" translates several words. *Sheol* in Hebrew and *Hades* in Greek usually mean the shadowy place of the dead in general. The word Jesus used most for final judgement was *Gehenna*, named after a valley outside Jerusalem where children had once been sacrificed to other gods (Jeremiah 7:31).

Jesus spoke about this more than anyone else in the New Testament. He used several images: fire (Matthew 25:41), outer darkness (Matthew 8:12), destruction (Matthew 10:28) and being shut out from the joy of God's presence (Matthew 25:10-12; 2 Thessalonians 1:9). These pictures are hard to combine literally (fire and darkness do not usually go together), which is one reason Christians differ on what they describe. All agree that rejecting God is serious, and that our choices matter.

## The main views Christians hold

**Eternal conscious punishment.** This has been the majority view through most of church history, among Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant teachers alike. Those who finally reject God are separated from him forever. Supporters point to Matthew 25:46, where "eternal" describes both the life of the righteous and the punishment of the wicked. They see it as honouring the seriousness of sin and the dignity of human choice. C. S. Lewis pictured hell as God finally giving people what they insist on: a life without him.

**[[annihilationism|Annihilationism]], or conditional immortality.** On this view, those who reject God are raised and judged, and then cease to exist. The punishment is eternal in its result, not in ongoing suffering. Supporters note that the Bible often speaks of the wicked perishing or being destroyed (John 3:16; Matthew 10:28), that the wages of sin is death (Romans 6:23), and that only God is immortal by nature (1 Timothy 6:16). It is a minority view, though the respected evangelical John Stott held it tentatively.

**The hope of universal reconciliation.** Some Christians hope that in the end God's love will win every person, perhaps through judgement as a purifying fire. They point to passages about God reconciling all things (Colossians 1:19-20), every knee bowing (Philippians 2:10-11) and God being all in all (1 Corinthians 15:28). Gregory of Nyssa, a 4th-century bishop, held this hope. Most churches have not accepted it as teaching, and a version linked with Origen was condemned in the 6th century. Critics say it does not take Jesus' warnings seriously enough. Some, such as the Catholic theologian Hans Urs von Balthasar, have argued that Christians may at least hope and pray for the salvation of all, without claiming to know.

## Catholic and Orthodox emphases

The [[catholic|Catholic]] Church teaches that hell is real and eternal, describing it above all as people freely and finally shutting themselves off from God. It teaches that God predestines no one to hell, and it has never declared any particular person to be there. Catholics also believe in [[purgatory]]: not a second chance or a halfway house to hell, but a final purification for people who die in God's grace and friendship but are not yet fully made holy, all of whom will reach heaven. Catholics point to passages such as 1 Corinthians 3:12-15 and 2 Maccabees 12:43-46, and pray for the dead.

[[orthodox|Orthodox]] Christians also pray for the dead, but they do not accept the Catholic teaching on purgatory as usually defined, and speak with reverent reserve about the state after death. Many Orthodox teachers, following Isaac the Syrian in the 7th century, describe heaven and hell as two experiences of the same love of God: to those who love him it is light and joy, and to those who reject him it burns.

Most [[protestant|Protestants]] do not accept purgatory either, believing that Christians go straight to be with Christ when they die, trusting in his finished work (Luke 23:43; Philippians 1:23).

## Justice and mercy together

Abraham once asked, "Will not the Judge of all the earth do right?" (Genesis 18:25). Whatever view you come to, that question is a good place to rest. God knows every heart far better than we do. He takes no pleasure in anyone's death (Ezekiel 33:11), and he is patient, not wanting anyone to perish (2 Peter 3:9). At the cross, justice and mercy meet. God does not ignore evil, but takes its weight on himself (Romans 3:25-26).

## Holding this well

- **If you are afraid for yourself,** remember that there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1), and that perfect love drives out fear (1 John 4:18).
- **If you are grieving someone,** you do not know everything that passed between that person and God. You can entrust them to a God who is more merciful than any of us.
- **If you talk about this with others,** do so with humility and compassion, never as a threat. Many thoughtful people, including people of other faiths and none, find this subject a real obstacle, and their concerns deserve a careful hearing.

Christians disagree about some details, but together they confess that God is just, that God is love, and that Jesus is the hope of the world.
